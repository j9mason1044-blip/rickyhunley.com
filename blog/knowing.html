Seven lessons

Knowing is not enough, we must apply

You can coast on talent and still look good enough to fool everybody but yourself.

Knowing is not enough, we must apply. Willing is not enough, we must do. — Bruce Lee

When I was coming up, my teammates never knew when I was playing below my max. Nobody could tell. That’s the trap. You can coast on talent and still look good enough to fool everybody but yourself.

My mom used to say, “What doesn’t kill you only makes you stronger.” I believed her, but believing isn’t the same as living it out on the field, in the classroom, in the office.

Here’s what I’ve learned: the hard work it takes to be the best at anything shows up disguised as work. It doesn’t announce itself. It just looks like one more rep, one more phone call, one more extra hour when everyone else has gone home. If you’re waiting for it to feel like destiny, you’ll miss it every time.

I grew up broke and hungry in Petersburg, Virginia. That feeling — the edge, the chip on your shoulder — never fully goes away, and it shouldn’t. Use it. Don’t get comfortable being a minimum-effort person. Your minimum will already look better than a lot of people’s maximum, and that’s exactly the trap that keeps good players from becoming great ones.

Attack. Be relentless. No exceptions, no excuses. Don’t let “cool with average” become your standard.

Knowing what to do has never been the hard part. Doing it — especially when nobody’s watching and nobody would ever know — that’s the whole game.
